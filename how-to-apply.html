Before you apply

How to apply, step by step

The application is long. That is deliberate, and it is not a test you can fail by being honest. Here is every section, and why it is there.

Joseph’s Home · Gulfport, Mississippi · 3 min read · updated August 2026

“Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.”

Proverbs 23:23 · KJV

Before you begin, know two things. First, you can save it and come back — there is a Save for later button at the bottom. Second, nothing in it disqualifies you automatically. Men have been accepted here with charges, with probation, with terrible employment histories. What ends an application is not a hard past. It is a dishonest one.

What to have in front of you

  • The last four digits of your Social Security number, and your date of birth
  • A phone number and email you actually check
  • Names and rough dates for the treatment centers you have been through
  • Your current medications, dosages, and prescribing doctor
  • Employment history, as best you remember it
  • Probation dates and your officer’s information, if you are on probation
  • Your church, if you have one, and your pastor’s name
  • Two people who will speak for you and take a phone call

Section by section

Your contacts

We ask for a name, phone, email and relationship, and whether that person has consented to be contacted. These are the people we will call when we need someone who knows you. Put down people who will answer the phone, not people you wish would.

Treatment center history

Where you went, when, and the reason for discharge. Men are tempted to leave off the ones that ended badly. Do not. A discharge that went wrong tells us more about what you need than three that went well.

Medications

Name, dosage, frequency, prescriber. This is a housing and safety question, not a judgment. Bring your prescriptions and keep your doctor. Any question about your medication is between you, your prescriber, and the staff — not something to be settled by strangers on the internet.

Family, employment, probation

We ask about family members because families are part of what we do; about employment because men here work; about probation because we would rather know than find out. If you are on probation, say so plainly and give us the dates.

Sex offender status

Answer it honestly. A yes is not automatically a no from us, but a lie discovered later is.

Testimony, and why we should choose you

The two written questions. They matter more than anything else on the form, and they have their own piece: what to write when we ask for your testimony.

The rules, the staff question, and your signature

You will be asked whether you have read and accept the house rules, and whether you will submit to the staff and be respectful. Read the rules first — they are a separate form — and then answer. Your electronic signature is legally binding, the same as ink.

After you submit, a member of staff will get in touch. If a week goes by and you have heard nothing, call and leave a message. Systems fail, phones die, and no one here will think less of you for following up.

The next step

If you are ready, the door is open.

The application asks a great deal of you, and it is meant to. Take it slowly, tell the truth, and save it as you go. If you get stuck, call and leave a message and someone will call you back.

For churches: how to walk alongside a home like this

Most churches want to help and are not sure what would actually be useful. This is the list.

Joseph’s Home · Gulfport, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“I was a stranger, and ye took me in.”

Matthew 25:35 · KJV

The most valuable thing you have is not money

It is ordinary membership. A man who is welcomed into a congregation — greeted by name, put on a work crew, invited to somebody’s table on a Sunday — has been given something no program can manufacture. That costs a church nothing except attention.

Things that genuinely help

  • A pew and a name. Learn his name, use it, ask him about his week, notice when he is not there.
  • Rides. Transport is one of the most persistent practical barriers in a house like this.
  • Work. If anyone in your congregation hires, our men are drug tested and accountable, and they cannot afford to be careless.
  • Mentors. Men in your church who will meet with a man weekly for a year, and who will not disappear in month three.
  • A meal, hosted. Not dropped off — hosted, at a table, with your family.
  • Skills. Somebody who will teach budgeting, or how to change brake pads, or how to talk to a landlord.
  • Household goods when a man moves into his own place at the end of the year. Call and ask what is needed rather than guessing.

Things to be careful with

Money direct to a man. Please talk to staff first. It is well meant and it frequently goes wrong.

Making him the testimony. A man in his fourth month does not need to be on a platform telling his story to a full sanctuary. Let him be a member for a while before he is an illustration.

Short-term intensity. A church that adopts the home enthusiastically for six weeks and vanishes does more harm than one that does one small thing faithfully for three years.

If you are a pastor

Our application asks every man for his church affiliation and his pastor’s name. If your name comes up, expect a call. And if you have a man in your congregation who needs this, call us before it becomes a crisis — 228-669-4346.

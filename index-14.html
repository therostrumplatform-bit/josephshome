We exist to disciple men to become who God created them to be.

We are named for Joseph Watts. His family called him Joey. Addiction took him, and the people who loved him decided his name would mean something for other men.

We believe the answers to what has been eating you are found in scripture and in the company of men who will not let you drift. We believe a man who dedicates himself to being discipled will live a life of godliness — and that clean and sober follows from that, rather than the other way round.

Ninety days was never going to do it.

Most men arrive having spent ten or fifteen years building the thing they are trying to leave. Thirty days interrupts it. Three years replaces it.

MONTHS 1–3

Getting your feet under you

A bed, a schedule, chores, church, and men who arrived before you and will tell you how this works. Week three is the hard one, and it is the same for everybody.

MONTHS 4–12

Work, church, and being known

A job with a local employer who knows the house. A congregation where you are a brother rather than a case. Celebrate Recovery, where the fight gets named out loud.

YEARS 2–3

Mentorship

You leave the house. You do not leave the men. This is the part most programs do not have, and it is the part that decides things.

More than a roof.

Education

A solid biblical foundation, plus GED and college preparation through community learning programs where it applies.

Work

We partner with local employers on the coast to match the right man with the right job. Work is part of the discipleship, not a way to pay us back.

Health

Basic care many of us take for granted, through every community program available to our men, and a push toward healthy habits and physical activity.

Church

Church and church activities are mandatory, alongside Celebrate Recovery at Michael Memorial and the Grace Point Campus.

Community

Men who hold each other accountable, and service projects that earn a man his way back into good standing in his own town.

Family

Our Hope ministry takes a whole-family approach, with weekend activities that care for the families of the men who live here.

We also serve alongside Navajo Missions — Bible classes, construction, food and clothing. Men are often surprised to find that being useful to somebody else is what finally convinces them they are not a lost cause.

Joseph's Home is a small operation on purpose. We are not a facility with an intake department and a waiting queue. When you write to us, you are writing to people who will know your name.

We take men who have finished rehab and who are ready to submit to something — a schedule, a house, staff, and ultimately Christ. We are honest with men about what this costs them and honest with families about what we can and cannot promise. If we are not the right place for you, we will say so and help you find the one that is.

Who was Joseph Watts?

His family called him Joey. To some he was Jojo, which he hated. To some he was Yofus. He was a son, a brother, a grandson, a cousin, a nephew and a friend. He was a fisherman and an avid outdoorsman. His humor and his laugh were contagious. He loved hard and he was a loyal friend.

Every man who walks through this door is here because of him.
